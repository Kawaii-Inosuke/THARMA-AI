National Workshop on Ethics of AI in eGovernance: 
Trust, Fairness, and Accountability Over Time 
 
ICSSR_Funded Research Project Event 
5-6 February, 2026 | SRM Institute of Science and Technology, Kattankulathur 

The Department of Computing Technologies, School of Computing, at SRM Institute 
of Science and Technology, hosted a two-day National Workshop on “Ethics of AI in 
eGovernance: Trust, Fairness, and Accountability Over Time," supported by the 
Indian Council of Social Science Research (ICSSR) under its funded research 
initiative. 

The workshop brought together enthusiastic participants, including faculty members, 
research scholars, students, policy practitioners, and industry professionals. The 
hybrid format enabled discussions on the ethical, regulatory, and technical dimensions 
of AI in public governance systems.

Key Themes and Deliberations 
The workshop facilitated interdisciplinary discussions on the evolving ethics of AI in 
digital governance. They moved beyond abstract principles and examined structural, 
institutional, and ethical implications of AI deployment in public systems over time. 

A recurring theme was the creation of institutional trust in AI-mediated governance 
systems. Trust is not merely a technical property. It is a socio-technical outcome 
shaped by transparency in decision logic, legibility of algorithmic outputs, fairness in 
automated decision-making, and citizen grievance-redressal mechanisms. Discussions 
highlighted that trust deficits often arise when AI systems are perceived as opaque or 
unchallengeable. 

They scrutinized how algorithmic systems may reproduce or amplify structural 
inequalities when trained on historically biased datasets. Speakers stressed that 
fairness in governance cannot be reduced to statistical metrics alone; it must 
incorporate constitutional values, justice principles, and socio-cultural realities and 
heed the risk of digital exclusion in automated governance processes. 

The emphasis was on ensuring that AI systems remain aligned with public values as 
they evolve, reaffirming the importance of sustained ethical monitoring rather than 
one-time compliance checks. Emerging governance models for AI oversight, 
sector-specific regulation in eGovernance, and policy frameworks must balance 
technological innovation with safeguards against algorithmic discrimination and legal 
transparency. 

The necessity of interdisciplinary collaboration was reasserted. Ethical AI in 
governance requires expertise spanning computer science, data engineering, public 
policy, political theory, law and constitutional studies, sociology, and development 
studies. The participation of scholars and practitioners from diverse domains 
reinforced the understanding that AI in eGovernance is fundamentally a 
socio-technical challenge. 

Conclusion 
The ICSSR-funded National Workshop affirmed that embedding trust, fairness, and 
accountability in eGovernance systems is not a static compliance exercise but an 
ongoing governance process. Ethical AI must be designed, monitored, evaluated, and 
recalibrated continuously to ensure alignment with democratic values and social 
justice objectives. By highlighting trust, fairness, and long-term accountability, the 
initiative restated the institution’s commitment to advancing responsible innovation 
and socially grounded AI research.
