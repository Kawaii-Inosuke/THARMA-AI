Major Issues of Artificial Intelligence in Security, 
Media, Responsibility and E-Governance. 
 
In the Chief Guest address, Air Marshal Mateshwaran speech, addressed the breakneck pace 
of development of Artificial Intelligence and its growing significance in the contemporary security 
and war. He raised the example of systems of defense, surveillance, and strategy making under 
the impact of AI technologies. 
 
Air Marshal Mateshwaran believes that AI can contribute to significant advancement of national 
security by enhancing its intelligence analysis, automation, and predictivity potential. 
Nevertheless, he also brought significant issues with the morality aspect of the use of AI in 
warfare. The autonomous systems which are used in military operations cast serious doubts on 
accountability, control by human beings, and safeguarding of human rights. 
 
He also pointed out that as much as AI has a strong ability to offer high-level technological 
benefits, the ethical accountability of such use should be thought about by governments and 
organizations. The issue of striking the equilibrium between technological progress and 
preservation of basic human values is a hot topic in the shaping of AI-based defence systems. 
 
Keynote on AI in Media 
 
In his keynote address, Nagaraj Nagabushnam from The Hindu Group discussed the growing 
role of Artificial Intelligence in modern media and journalism.. 
 
He has mentioned AI as a not-seen-bureaucrat that operates behind the scenes in online media 
outlets. There is increased application of AI systems in organizing, recommending, and curating 
content to readers. The systems are based on algorithms, which assign the promotion or 
highlighting of the news stories based on the behavior of users, the metrics of engagement and 
reading. 
 
Mr. Nagabushnam clarified that, although AI is capable of bringing efficiency and personalization 
to the media platforms, transparency and accountability are also critical concerns that need to 
be addressed. As algorithms determine the information audiences are exposed to, there is a 
necessity to make sure that these systems remain fair and do not adversely impact such a 
course of misinformation and bias dissemination. 
 
He pointed out that AI should be used in responsible ways in the field of journalism, and editorial 
supervision should be retained as well as making the algorithmic systems transparent and 
answerable to the audience. 
 
 

 



 
AI in Global South Responsible. 
 
Papia S. Gupta from Jawaharlal Nehru University addressed the challenges of developing 
Responsible Artificial Intelligence, particularly in the context of the Global South. 
 
Dr. Gupta emphasized that a significant number of AI technologies are created with references 
to the datasets and frames constructed in Western nations. These systems might not 
necessarily indicate social, cultural, and economic realities of the developing areas. 
 
She has highlighted that she would focus on the creation of trustful AI systems that focus on 
fairness, transparency, and inclusiveness. She writes that to be a responsible AI, one needs to 
pay attention to ethical practice, data privacy, and social concerns of algorithmic decisions. 
 
Another thing that Dr. Gupta emphasized is the necessity of policymakers, researchers, and the 
developers of AI systems to work together and make sure that these systems can be utilized 
positively by society at large. It is necessary to create ethical standards and regulations that 
would oversee the development of AI to make it aligned to the common interest and social 
justice. 
 
AI in Public Services and E-Governance. 
 
Nila Ganguli from Guru Nanak College discussed the application of Artificial Intelligence in 
e-governance and public administrationAccording to Dr. Ganguli, digital governance systems 
will have a great potential in enhancing the service delivery process by minimizing paperwork, 
enhancing efficiency of administration and allowing the citizens to access services remotely. 
Nevertheless, she also identified some possible obstacles like algorithmic bias, which can take 
place due to the incomplete or prejudiced datasets on which AI systems are based. 
 
To mitigate these issues, she declared the need to create inclusive digital systems that would 
make equal access to all citizens and most importantly marginalized communities. Close 
attention and proper ethical management should be involved to make sure that AI-services in 
the public sector will be equal, transparent, and responsible. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

 



 
Final Reflections on Artificial Intelligence and Society 
 
Air Marshal Mateshwaran, Nagaraj Nagabushnam, Papia S. Gupta, and Nila Ganguli have all, in 
their separate discourses, collectively unfolded the story of how Artificial Intelligence is gradually 
dominant in various spheres of the human society. AI is becoming increasingly indispensable in 
the modern, day technological landscape, be it for national security and warfare, media 
systems, governance, or global ethical frameworks. 
 
A datum in all presentations was the critical leverage of technological innovation by ethical 
considerations. Despite the fact that AI can bring about remarkable improvements like increased 
productivity, excellent data analytics, and enhanced decision, making capabilities, it can also 
give rise to issues of human rights, openness, responsibility, and justice. To give a few 
examples, the security and media realms cannot afford to overlook the necessity of vigilant 
oversight and prudent governance as far as the possible ways of abuse and/or collateral 
damages with AI systems are concerned. 
 
As well, the talk on #ResponsibleAI# in the Global South points to the necessity for a collectively 
beneficial tech development that is socially and culturally aware. The PC transformation of 
governance is another example where, on the one hand, digital tools can facilitate public service 
delivery, but on the other hand, there is a pressing issue of preventing them from being vehicles 
of bias and inequality. 
 
In sum, these meetings have cast light on the fact that AI's future will be a garden where ethical 
petals, robust regulatory fences, and continuous human caretaking will be the components that 
make technology flourish in a way that society as a whole reaps the benefits.
