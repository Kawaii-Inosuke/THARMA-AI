THE QUIET RISE OF AI 
Reflections on Technology, Ethics and Society 
 
 
 
Artificial Intelligence is something that people think is away from us.. The truth is, 
Artificial Intelligence is already a part of our daily lives. Artificial Intelligence unlocks 
our phone recommending the movies we watch, filters the content we see online and 
helps governments manage services and make decisions. 
 
When we listen to experts talk about Artificial Intelligence the conversation is not 
about technology. It is about ethics, responsibility, power and the future of society. 
 
I listened to speakers, including Dr. Nagaraj Nagabhushanam, Dr. Papia Sengupta, 
Dr. Neela Ganguly Sundaram and Mr. Shivam. They made me realize that the story 
of Artificial Intelligence is not about machines. It is about how humans choose to 
design and use those machines. 
 
 
Artificial Intelligence as an " Bureaucrat” 
 
One idea that stood out was described by Dr. Nagaraj Nagabhushanam. He said that 
Artificial Intelligence is becoming like a " bureaucrat." 
 
Traditionally governments had administrators who reviewed files, analyzed data and 
made decisions. Now governments use dashboards, automated systems and 
Artificial Intelligence-powered tools to process information and support 
decision-making. 
 
This change has advantages. Artificial Intelligence systems can process a lot of data, 
quickly detect patterns that humans might miss and help administrators monitor 
services more efficiently. 
 
This also raises a concern. If algorithms influence decisions that affect people’s lives, 
such as welfare distribution, service access or public policies then we need to think 
about accountability and transparency. 
 
Technology can help governance. It should not replace human judgment completely. 
Ethical oversight and human responsibility must remain at the center of 
decision-making. 
 
 

 



 

When Technology Reflects Human Bias 
 
Dr. Papia Sengupta shared another perspective. She talked about the challenges 
surrounding Artificial Intelligence. 
 
Many people think that technology is neutral.. Artificial Intelligence systems are 
trained using data generated by human societies and those societies already have 
biases and inequalities. 
 
If biased data is used to train Artificial Intelligence systems the technology may. Even 
amplify those biases. This can happen in areas such as hiring processes, financial 
services, healthcare and law enforcement. 
 
Instead of reducing inequality, poorly designed Artificial Intelligence systems could 
make existing social problems worse. 
 
This makes it clear that developing Artificial Intelligence is not a technical challenge. 
It is also an ethical responsibility. 
 
 
The Data We Give Away Without Thinking 
 
Dr. Neela Ganguly Sundaram shared a thought-provoking insight. She talked about 
the role that personal data plays in today’s digital world. 
 
Every day people install apps, create accounts and agree to terms and conditions 
without fully understanding what they are accepting. Most of us rarely read the 
privacy policies before clicking "accept.” 
 
However these agreements often allow companies and platforms to collect 
information about our behavior, preferences and interactions. 
 
Over time this data becomes very valuable. It enables systems to analyze patterns, 
predict user behavior and shape experiences. 
 
This raises a question about data ownership and privacy. In a world where data 
powers digital technologies it becomes crucial to ask who controls that data and how 
it is being used. 
 
 
 
 
 

 



 

The Hidden Work Behind Artificial Intelligence Systems 
 
Mr. Shivam offered a perspective on how Artificial Intelligence systems are built. 
 
One surprising insight is that developing Artificial Intelligence is not about designing 
complex algorithms. In reality a significant portion of the work involves 
collecting,organizing and preparing data. 
 
Artificial Intelligence systems rely heavily on information, metadata and knowledge 
bases to understand context and produce meaningful results. Without well-organized 
data even the most advanced algorithms struggle to function effectively. 
 
This reminds us that Artificial Intelligence is not some magical technology. Behind 
every system is a lot of human effort in managing data and building knowledge 
systems. 
 
 
The Real Challenge: Responsible Innovation 
 
After hearing these perspectives one idea became clear: the most important 
question about Artificial Intelligence is not just what the technology can do but how 
responsibly we choose to use it. 
 
Artificial Intelligence has the potential to improve efficiency, simplify processes and 
support better decision-making. At the time it also introduces risks related to privacy, 
bias, transparency and accountability. 
 
The challenge for societies today is to find the balance—embracing technological 
innovation while ensuring that ethical principles guide its development. 
 
Artificial Intelligence should strengthen capabilities rather than replace them. It 
should make systems more transparent and inclusive, rather than more difficult to 
understand. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

 



 

 
Looking Ahead 
 
Artificial Intelligence will continue to evolve and shape the world around us.. Its 
impact will depend largely on the choices we make today—how we design 
systems,how we regulate technology and how society decides to use these powerful 
tools. 
 
The insights shared by the speakers made one thing very clear: Artificial Intelligence 
should remain a tool created by humans to improve life, not a system that quietly 
shapes society without accountability. 
 
Because in the end technology should serve people—not the way around.
